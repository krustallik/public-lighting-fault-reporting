# P2c security and privacy evidence — geolocation and reverse geocoding

**Status:** Canonical external security/privacy evidence for P2c research. This is evidence and design input, not a legal assessment or an accepted product decision.

**Evidence date:** 2026-10-05 (Europe/Bratislava). Official web sources were rechecked on this date. Their policies and standards may change; recheck them before implementation.

**Repository baseline:** `master` at `c11235f7a70822b354d27d49b618d181a37a133e`.

**Related checkpoint:** [`p2c-mobile-reporting-geolocation-privacy-plan.md`](../checkpoints/p2c-mobile-reporting-geolocation-privacy-plan.md).

## 1. Scope and limits

This note records official browser, provider, accessibility, and legal-source material relevant to optional browser geolocation, map-selected locations, and reverse geocoding. It does not determine lawful basis, controller/processor roles, whether any precise location in a particular report is personal data, required notices, retention periods, or whether a specific provider may be used for citizen-derived coordinates. Product/provider/privacy choices require owner decisions; legal applicability requires a separate qualified legal review against the actual deployment and data flow.

No geolocation capability or user-location reverse-geocode flow is implemented in the inspected frontend. No citizen-location or report data is sent to AUSEMIO by the current product flow. The reported browser and provider behaviors below are external evidence, not repository behavior.

### Design implications grounded in these sources

1. Keep geolocation optional. The browser permission prompt is not product consent to every downstream transfer. Disclose and gate any send of coordinates to the backend or an external geocoder.
2. A reverse-geocoded string is a suggestion, not proof of the address at the coordinate. The user must be able to inspect and correct it, consistent with the accepted P2c product requirement.
3. Never call public Nominatim on every map movement or as autocomplete. If retained, requests should be explicit and limited, and every call path must share application-wide rate coordination for the public service.
4. The OSMF policy's ban on sending personal/confidential data creates an unresolved provider and product/privacy gate for coordinates obtained from a citizen's device or tied to a report. Any applicable legal review is a separate gate; neither provider terms nor this evidence note determines legal compliance. Do not infer that a coordinate is non-personal merely because it identifies a public asset.
5. Geolocation's accuracy is an estimate, not a guaranteed error bound around a lighting fault. Do not silently equate the device position with the fault location.
6. Automated axe results alone cannot establish WCAG conformance; combine automated checks with keyboard, screen-reader, touch, zoom/reflow, and human review.
7. **Current public-endpoint admission risk:** `GET /api/geocode/reverse` is mounted without route-level authentication, throttling, or admission control in repository code. The code does not decide whether it should remain anonymous/public, serve only an intended product flow, or be a general public API. Record caller scope, admission, bounded backlog, overload/rejection and fairness as the separate owner gate O11 in the [P2c plan](../checkpoints/p2c-mobile-reporting-geolocation-privacy-plan.md); do not treat a future queue as safe unless its capacity and rejection contract are bounded and owner-selected. Deployment proxy controls remain unknown.

## 4. Candidate observability (not approved)

No new per-request citizen-location event or database field is recommended by this evidence note. If reliability/policy monitoring later requires telemetry, the smallest candidate is aggregate counters/histograms such as provider identifier, outcome class, cache hit/miss, queue wait-duration bucket, request-duration bucket, and coarse time bucket. These are a proposal only and should not include coordinates, address, request URL/query, report fields, contact data, exact user identifier, or a stable per-report ID.

| Candidate field/group | Purpose / necessity | Identifiability risk | Retention question | Access / delete implications |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Provider identifier + outcome class | Determine whether a configured provider is available and classify success/no-result/timeout/upstream refusal; operationally useful if external geocoding is approved. | Low in aggregate; a rare outcome and precise event timestamp can still correlate with activity. | Owner/legal reviewer must set a short operational period and aggregation/deletion schedule. | Restrict to operations maintainers; deleting raw series may be possible, but backups/monitoring vendor retention must be checked. |
| Cache hit/miss + queue-wait/request-duration buckets | Validate aggregate load and rate coordination without retaining query coordinates. | Low in aggregate; exact timestamps or per-request rows raise correlation risk. | Prefer short-lived process metrics or coarse aggregated windows; exact period is an owner decision. | No citizen-level lookup should exist, so user-specific deletion is not applicable; operators must still remove expired aggregate data from telemetry systems. |

If the operational requirement can be met by in-memory counters that reset at process restart, that is preferable to adding a persistent event table before purpose and retention are settled. Any materially richer telemetry must be separately reviewed and approved.

## 5. Questions for owner/legal review

- Is any device-derived coordinate sent to the app backend or a third party, and at what exact user-confirmed point in the flow?
- Does the chosen reverse-geocoding provider accept citizen-derived/fault-location coordinates under its current policy and contractual/privacy terms? OSMF public Nominatim explicitly says not to submit personal/confidential material; resolve this before provider approval.
- Which location is authoritative for the report: inventory asset ID/coordinates, user-confirmed map point, selected address/locality, or a documented combination? How are conflicts resolved?
- May a selected address/coordinate be cached at all? What precision, TTL, scope, provider version/language key, and deletion path are justified?
- Is any report, contact, location or attachment data retained or logged outside application source code by hosting, reverse proxies, monitoring, backups, analytics, tile/geocoder providers, or AUSEMIO? Obtain deployment evidence before making retention claims.
- Which lawful basis, purpose/notice, controller/processor arrangement, data-subject access/erasure process, retention and any impact assessment are applicable under the effective GDPR and Slovak rules? **OWNER + QUALIFIED LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED.**
- **Owner decision O11 (separate from legal review):** who/what may invoke `/api/geocode/reverse`, whether it remains anonymous/public, whether it is limited to the intended product flow or is a general public API, and what application-boundary admission, fairness, bounded backlog, overload/rejection and upstream coordination contract applies? Exact policy values and mechanisms remain undecided in the canonical P2c plan.
